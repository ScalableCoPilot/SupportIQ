## User-role observations

- API authorization, tenant scope, and least privilege are explicit in the baseline.
- Roles may be seeded for a demo but should remain explicit and testable.
- The baseline does not provide a role-permission matrix or tenant membership model. The project owner confirmed external customers are assistant users and limited their retrieval to publicly approved knowledge; their identity and conversation-history rules remain open.
- The project owner assigned external knowledge approval to a Support Manager or explicitly authorized higher-level role and confirmed content-quality/confidentiality criteria. Detailed invitation/approval workflow remains deferred to design.
- The owner confirmed Support Agent and Support Manager knowledge scopes and stated tenant isolation must always be enforced. The criteria for knowledge relevance to a Support Agent's role remain open.
- The owner confirmed the baseline's categorical prohibition: Platform Admin must not access any tenant business content. The owner also specified permitted platform-level metadata and operational diagnostics; detailed operational field rules remain to be validated.
- The owner specified the platform-level metadata categories Platform Admin may access and explicitly excluded tenant business content, customer data, tickets, knowledge articles, and uploaded documents.
- The Platform Admin boundary is the clearest explicit restriction and should be preserved during discovery.
- The project owner confirmed customers are intended assistant users; their permitted knowledge, conversation history, identity, and tenant/customer scope remain open in [open-questions.md](../evidence/open-questions.md).

## Discovery plan

Gate 1 is complete. In Gate 2, the project owner confirmed both internal support staff and external customers are assistant audiences, local-first development/testing followed by a hosted Azure demo, Azure SQL for both local-development and hosted environments, and Azure AI Foundry for answer generation and embeddings. Exact model, region, and cost-envelope decisions are deferred to detailed design; deployment configuration remains open. The owner also confirmed Tenant Admin invitation/provisioning of internal support staff accounts and read/manage access to all knowledge lifecycle statuses in the tenant. Tenant Admins and Support Agents may create/edit external articles for Support Manager review but may not approve, reject, or publish them; those authorities remain with Support Managers or explicitly authorized higher-level roles. The owner confirmed JWT claims for UserId, UserName, TenantId, Role, and expiration (`exp`), with TenantId and Role validated server-side for authorization; further permission/claim details may be finalized during design. The owner confirmed limited external retrieval to explicitly approved and published knowledge meeting stated quality and confidentiality criteria, and approval/lifecycle actions by Support Managers. The owner reaffirmed that Platform Admins may not access tenant business content, specified allowed platform-level operational metadata, selected knowledge management plus RAG assistance as the must-have first-release vertical slice, and deferred ticket list/detail, summary/classification, simple analytics, read-only lookup tools, synthetic ticket taxonomy/fields/seed strategy, and GitHub issue/PR, CI, and delivery workflow requirements to future phases. Support Agents may create/read/manage all internal knowledge in their own tenant, independent of uploader, retrieve it in all lifecycle statuses, and by default retrieve published external knowledge unless role- or permission-based restrictions are configured; they also view/respond to escalated external customer conversations. Support Managers may manage those restrictions and retrieve all tenant knowledge statuses; tenant isolation must always be enforced. No additional first-release role/permission boundaries were requested; permissions for out-of-scope capabilities remain deferred. Outdated articles are withdrawn until reapproved, with review triggers, audit behavior, and a configurable 90-day default review interval with per-article override. Document retention starts on original upload; the source document, extracted text, chunks, and embeddings are deleted after 90 days, while backup deletion and log/evaluation-record retention remain open. No first-release AI/RAG evaluation thresholds are set; measurable metrics, datasets, and acceptance thresholds are to be defined during detailed design before release approval. No numeric latency, availability/recovery, concurrency, or operating-cost targets are confirmed; measurable targets are to be defined during detailed design and performance testing before release approval. First-release support is English-only, with keyboard navigation, readable contrast, labels, and screen-reader-friendly controls; WCAG conformance remains open for detailed design. API schemas, paths, validation, pagination, idempotency, and error formats are deferred to solution/detailed design; authentication, tenant isolation, role authorization, secure knowledge access, and consistent source/derived deletion remain requirements. Membership actions and audit requirements are confirmed; detailed invitation/approval workflows and GitHub issue/PR, CI, and delivery workflow requirements remain deferred.

The project owner approved the Gate 2 discovery findings and the Gate 3 requirements package as presented, then approved the PRD at Gate 4 with Q-033 remaining open. Requirements explicitly marked Approved at Gate 3 are approved; baseline-only items retained as Draft/unprioritized and unresolved/deferred decisions retain those statuses. Implementation planning remains unauthorized pending Gate 5.
